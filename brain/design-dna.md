# The design DNA — extracted from the reference library

Adrian's answer to "what does good look like" was 29 newspapers rather than one.
That turns out to be more useful, because the things they **share** are the real
standard. Fourteen studied closely so far; the library is at `/library.html`.

## The seven rules they all obey

**1. One loud thing per page. Everything else is quiet and dense.**
Every single reference does this. The Barcelona spread makes it explicit — a full
poster page facing a page of tight text and small ads. Never two loud pages
facing each other. Never two quiet ones. *This is the rule our pages break most.*

**2. Illustration, not photography, carries the big moments.**
Le Cafetier, Arkitektnytt, Troika, Die Zeit, Süddeutsche, NYT Sunday Review,
Boston Globe — all illustration-led. Photography appears only where the object
itself is the point (a product, a player). Illustration is also what keeps a page
from reading as generated.

**3. Flat colour fields, not decorated boxes.**
The NYT Sunday Review page is a flat coral field with a drawing on top. The
Packers page is a flat green field with a cut-out figure. The colour is the
*ground*, not a highlight behind a headline. The one template-looking reference
in the set (Journal) fails precisely because its colour is decoration.

**4. Colour is information.** Section labels, folios and jump lines carry the
department ink. Troika puts the section word in colour in the folio; Le Cafetier
uses red for nothing except labels and page numbers. If a colour isn't telling
you where you are, it shouldn't be there.

**5. Density earns its rest — and rest earns its density.**
Boston Globe puts an illustrated scene and a giant numeral at the top, then five
columns of genuinely dense listings underneath. The dense part is *comfortable*
because the top gave the eye somewhere to stand. Our pages are often uniformly
medium, which reads as flat.

**6. The furniture is never skipped.** Folio with section name, page number,
date. Kicker above the headline. Byline between rules. Deck. Caption. Credit.
Jump line. These are what make a page read as a newspaper rather than a poster
with columns — and they cost nothing.

**7. Headlines sit inside the artwork.**
Redbook, Le Cafetier's final cover, Die Zeit, Packers, Boston Globe — the
headline overlaps or is enclosed by the image. Not stacked above it in a
separate band.

## Specific moves worth stealing

- **Teaser strips top and bottom** of the cover, four across, each with a
  coloured section label and a page number (Le Cafetier).
- **A white text panel floated on top of a full-bleed illustration** (Die Zeit).
- **Two illustration bands sandwiching a column of text**, headline dropped into
  the middle of the page (Süddeutsche).
- **A question set in a lighter tint inside the artwork** as a rest stop (NYT).
- **A giant numeral locked into the illustration** as the entry point (Globe).
- **Pill-shaped category labels** breaking a long list into sections (Globe).
- **A section slug with a fraction** — `culturas. 1/2` — so you know where you
  are inside a section (Barcelona).
- **A vertical rail** of small ads, stats or teasers down the outer edge
  (Barcelona, Packers, Die Zeit).
- **One diagonal object breaking the grid** — the grid reads as stronger for
  being broken exactly once (Chosun).
- **Duotone illustration in the section ink** — striking, cheap to print, and
  impossible to mistake for a generated image (Troika, Arkitektnytt).

## What this tells us to stop doing

- Stop putting a headline in a band above a picture.
- Stop making every page the same medium density.
- Stop using colour as a background behind text for no reason.
- Stop treating pages as independent — design the spread.
- Stop reaching for photography when an illustration would carry it.

## How to use this

When you build a page, name the reference you're answering and which of the
seven rules it leans on. A page that can't name either is a page nobody has
art-directed. Add new references to `public/refs/` and a row to
`public/data/refs.json` — and write down what to take, not just that you liked it.
