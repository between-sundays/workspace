# Page concepts from Adrian's curated inspiration

Words only, no visuals yet. Each entry: **what the piece actually does** (the
mechanic, not the look), then **our original version**, rooted in a named passage.
Nothing here forces existing copy.

---

## 01 · Apple, "To the crazy ones"
**The mechanic:** a full page of nothing but centred prose, ragged and stepped,
building by accumulation. No image does any work. The power is that a company
spent a full page saying something that isn't about its product.

**Ours — "To the ones who were not chosen."**
A full page listing the people God picked, each one disqualified on paper. The
youngest son out in the field. The murderer. The prostitute. The tax collector.
The one who denied him three times. Stepped and ragged like the Apple ad, ending
on the turn: *these are the ones he picked on purpose.*
**Root:** 1 Corinthians 1:27 — *"God chose things the world considers foolish in
order to shame those who think they are wise."*
**Where:** a right-hand page, no facing image. Page 03 or 27.

## 02 · Nike, "JUST ___ IT"
**The mechanic:** two enormous words hold the corners and the entire story is
crammed inside them at body size. You read the shout, then discover the whisper.

**Ours — "I AM ___ WITH YOU."**
Those four words at poster scale, and inside them, in 9pt, every place in
scripture God says it: to Jacob at Bethel, to Moses at the bush, to Joshua, to
Gideon hiding in a winepress, to Jeremiah who says he is too young, to the
disciples at the very end of Matthew. Dozens of instances, one after another,
filling the negative space.
**Root:** Genesis 28:15 · Exodus 3:12 · Joshua 1:9 · Judges 6:16 · Jeremiah 1:8
· Matthew 28:20.
**Where:** this is the poster page. Page 39.

## 03 · "12 most beautiful questions ever asked"
**The mechanic:** a plain numbered list, no design at all, that people screenshot
because every line lands. The typography is nothing; the selection is everything.

**Ours — "The questions Jesus asked."**
He asks about 300 and answers almost none. Set them plainly, numbered, no
commentary: *What do you want me to do for you? · Do you want to get well? · Who
do you say I am? · Why are you afraid? · Where is your faith? · Do you love me?*
Just the questions and their references. The absence of answers is the design.
**Root:** Mark 10:51 · John 5:6 · Matthew 16:15 · Matthew 8:26 · Luke 8:25 · John 21:17.
**Where:** page 16 or a right-hand page facing something loud.

---

## 04 · "How to journal when you don't know what to say"
**The mechanic:** a bulleted list that removes the excuse. Every bullet lowers the
bar rather than raising it.

**Ours — "How to pray when you don't know what to say."**
Same structure, same generosity, every line sourced. *Start with a complaint —
Psalm 13 does. · Say you don't believe and ask for help with that — Mark 9:24. ·
Use somebody else's words — the Psalms are borrowed prayers. · Say nothing;
Romans 8:26 says the Spirit does it for you. · Be angry out loud — Job never
apologises for it.*
**Root:** Romans 8:26 — *"the Holy Spirit prays for us with groanings that cannot
be expressed in words."*
**Where:** pairs with page 43, the notebook page.

---

## 05 · Weekly Bingo for tiny achievements
**The mechanic:** a hand-drawn 5×5 grid of things too small to count as
achievements, which is exactly why ticking them feels good.

**Ours — "The week you actually had."**
Twenty-five squares of ordinary faithfulness nobody claps for: *told the truth
when a lie was easier · let someone go first · said sorry first · didn't say the
thing · read one verse · sat with somebody who was sad · asked for help.*
Free square in the middle: **you woke up.**
**Root:** Zechariah 4:10 — *"Do not despise these small beginnings."*
**Where:** the games block, page 45.

---

## 06 · The Fixer's Manifesto / Incomplete Manifesto for Growth / Nike's 10 principles
**The mechanic:** numbered declaratives, no hedging, set as a wall of type. Reads
like something pinned above a workbench.

**Ours — "The Between Sundays Manifesto."**
Twelve numbered lines, each one a verse in plain speech with the reference set
small. *1. NOTHING IS DISQUALIFYING. Romans 8:38. 2. YOU DO NOT HAVE TO FIX
YOURSELF FIRST. Romans 5:8. 3. SMALL IS NOT NOTHING. Zechariah 4:10. 4. DOUBT IS
ALLOWED IN THE ROOM. Mark 9:24…*
**Where:** back-of-book, or the inside front.

## 07 · The Porch Light Theory (handwritten + highlighter)
**The mechanic:** handwriting makes it feel like it was meant for you, and the
highlighter shows you what the writer thought mattered — you're reading someone
else's emphasis.

**Ours — the marked-up page we already scoped.**
Someone's real handwriting, doubting and stressed, with six places where a single
word is crossed out or circled and an arrow runs to the correction, verse written
out in the margin in red. *"I'll come back when I've got it together"* → circled →
Matthew 11:28. *"I'm too far gone"* → cross out **too far** → Luke 15:20, and in
the margin: **he RAN.**
**Root:** Luke 15:20 · Matthew 11:28 · Romans 5:8 · Mark 9:24 · Genesis 28:15.
**Where:** page 05.

## 08 · Microsoft "We see" + the fence photo + the kitchen illustration
**The mechanic:** a real photograph with hand-drawn line art continuing out of it.
The drawing shows what isn't visible in the frame.

**Ours — "What else was in the room."**
Photograph an ordinary place — a stairwell, a kitchen at 11:40pm, a car park.
Then draw over it, in one continuous white line, what the text says is also
present: the ramp, the traffic going both ways, the ones going up and down.
Caption: *the photograph is real. So is the other thing.*
**Root:** Genesis 28:12 · 2 Kings 6:17 — *"the LORD opened the young man's eyes,
and he saw"* — the servant who could not see the army until his eyes were opened.
**Where:** the photo essay, page 44. This is what that page has been missing.

## 09 · Notes to Strangers (the envelope wall)
**The mechanic:** an object you take from a wall. Physical, anonymous, one-to-one.
It works because you didn't earn it and nobody watched you take it.

**Ours — a perforated page of tear-off notes.**
Twelve small notes down a page, each perforated, each with one line and its
reference, blank on the back. Instruction at the top: **take one, leave one, give
one away.** The paper becomes something that leaves the paper.
**Root:** Matthew 10:8 — *"Give as freely as you have received."*
**Where:** its own page, near the Spine so it can be torn out cleanly.

## 10 · "Perspective Matters" (three shapes, all labelled TRUTH)
**The mechanic:** three drawings of the same object from different angles, all
labelled the same word. The whole argument is made without a sentence.

**Ours — the same night, three ways.**
The identical scene drawn three times: *what Jacob saw* (a field, a rock, the
dark) · *what the people of Luz saw* (nothing; an ordinary Tuesday) · *what was
actually there* (the ramp, the traffic, all night). All three labelled
**BETHEL.**
**Root:** Genesis 28:16–17.
**Where:** page 15, the place you almost walked past.

## 11 · Emotions are Messengers / Where are you living? / Things I can control
**The mechanic:** a two- or three-column table that reframes a feeling as
information rather than a verdict. Cold layout, warm effect.

**Ours — "What it is / what you tell yourself / what is actually true."**
Three columns. *Afraid → I am on my own → Isaiah 41:10. Ashamed → I have to fix
it first → Romans 5:8. Exhausted → I should be able to do this → Matthew 11:28.
Numb → something is wrong with me → Psalm 42:5, a man asking himself why he is
so far down.*
**Where:** near the weather page, 20–21.

---

## 12 · The blackout / cut-out poetry (both versions)
**The mechanic:** an existing page destroyed until a new sentence surfaces. It
only works if the words are genuinely there — otherwise it is white text on bars.

**Ours — verified, not faked.** I searched eleven books for extractable lines.
Three work with a tight span:
- **"nothing can separate us from the love of God"** — Romans 8:38–39, 65 words
- **"you are not alone / I am with you"** — John 16:29–33, 73 words · *this is the
  issue title*
- **"come to me and I will give you rest"** — Matthew 11:28, 18 words

Adrian's line — *"falling in love with Jesus is the best thing I've ever done"* —
is **not** extractable from scripture: the only hit spans 5,382 words across John
11–17. It can still be the ad; it just cannot claim the Bible as its source text.
**Where:** full-page ad. Recommend the John 16 version.

## 13 · The conspiracy board (red string) + the canoe on a sea of words
**The mechanic:** overwhelming density with one small human figure or one clear
centre. The scale contrast is the whole feeling.

**Ours — "Everything he was told to carry."**
A full page of red-threaded connections between every promise made to Abraham,
Isaac and Jacob, showing they are the same promise handed down three times. One
small figure asleep at the centre with a rock under his head.
**Root:** Genesis 12:2–3 · 26:24 · 28:13–15 — the same words, three generations.
**Where:** a spread. This is a page nobody else in the category would make.

---

## 14 · "Banksy said / Matisse said" (handwritten quote grid)
**The mechanic:** a hand-lettered grid where each cell is one person saying one
thing. Democratic — no quote is bigger than another.

**Ours — "What people said in the dark."**
Hand-lettered grid, each cell one line from someone at their lowest, with the
reference. *Job said the day I was born should be erased. David said how long
must I wrestle with my thoughts. Elijah said I have had enough, take my life.
Jeremiah said I am mocked all day long. Jesus said my God, why have you
abandoned me.* Nothing resolved on the page.
**Root:** Job 3:3 · Psalm 13:2 · 1 Kings 19:4 · Jeremiah 20:7 · Matthew 27:46.
**Where:** facing something hopeful. Deliberately.

---

## 15 · 30-Day Challenge grid
**The mechanic:** a numbered grid, a blank line for what you're committing to, and
a joke reward at the bottom that lowers the stakes.

**Ours — "30 days. One chapter."**
Genesis 1–30 or a psalm a day. Thirty boxes to X off. Rewards at the bottom:
*□ a taco dinner □ you actually read a whole book of the Bible □ nothing, and
that's fine.*
**Root:** Joshua 1:8.
**Where:** with the Spine.

---

## 16 · "Once in a House on Fire" (matchbook cover)
**The mechanic:** one object photographed straight, with the drawing completing
the metaphor. Nothing else on the cover.

**Ours — a stone, photographed straight, with a drawn ladder rising out of it.**
Or a real matchbook where the matches are unstruck. One object, one idea, nothing
else on the page.
**Root:** Genesis 28:11 · Isaiah 42:3 — *"He will not crush the weakest reed or
put out a flickering candle."*
**Where:** a cover candidate.

---

## Rejected, and why

- **Dopamine Menu / study-tips / Hawthorne / Occam's / Eisenhower / 70-20-10 /
  Zero to Pro Strategist.** Useful for *us* internally, but they are
  self-optimisation content. Putting a productivity matrix in a faith paper makes
  us the thing we said we would not be — an expert handing down a system. Keep
  them in the workspace, not the paper.
- **The storytelling mind map.** A diagram about storytelling is not storytelling.

---

## The rule this whole exercise proves

Every strong piece above works because of a **mechanic**, not a look. Copy the
mechanic, source the content from the text, and it becomes ours. Copy the look
and it becomes a pastiche of somebody's Pinterest board.

# Batch two

## 17 · "A House in Memory" — cut-up words arranged into a shape
**The mechanic:** words cut from a book and physically arranged into the outline
of the thing the book is about. The form and the content are the same object.

**Ours — the stairway, built out of Genesis 28.**
Every word of the passage cut into strips and laid as a staircase rising off the
bottom-left corner, each step a phrase, the treads getting shorter as they climb.
Some strips upside down. Real scissors, real shadows, photographed flat.
**Root:** Genesis 28:12. **Where:** a cover candidate, or the poster page.
*This is the single strongest idea in either batch.*

## 18 · "300 Positive Words of Encouragement" — a wall of single words
**The mechanic:** hundreds of words in tiny columns. You don't read it, you scan
until one hits you. Density does the work.

**Ours — "What the Bible calls you."**
Every name scripture gives a person, in columns, each with its reference set
tiny: *chosen · kept · named · found · carried · known · called · redeemed ·
adopted · not forgotten · engraved on his palms.* Hundreds of them. Somewhere in
that wall is the one you needed.
**Root:** Isaiah 49:16 · 43:1 · Ephesians 1:5 · 1 Peter 2:9. **Where:** a full page.

## 19 · The design-thinking squiggle
**The mechanic:** one continuous line — a knot that gradually becomes straight.
No words needed. Everyone recognises their own life in it.

**Ours — the same line, labelled at four points.**
*Beersheba* at the tangled start · *the stone* in the middle of the knot ·
*Bethel* where it begins to straighten · *twenty years* along the straight part.
Caption: **most of this drawing is the part nobody writes down.**
**Root:** Genesis 28:10 — an 800km walk in one sentence. **Where:** page 27.

## 20 · The piano page — markup over typed text, object below
**The mechanic:** typed prose with hand circling, highlighting and blackout over
it, and a photograph of the object underneath. The markup is someone reacting in
real time; the object grounds it.

**Ours — the marked-up page, upgraded.** Same doubting-thoughts concept as
concept 07, but the object anchors it: a photograph of a plain rock, cut out on
white, sitting below the marked-up text. Nothing else on the page.
**Root:** Genesis 28:11. **Where:** page 05.

## 21 · "Alternatives to How are you?" — a grid of better questions
**The mechanic:** a hand-lettered grid replacing one useless question with
thirty-five usable ones. Immediately practical.

**Ours — "What to say when somebody is grieving."**
Grid of things that actually help, and — the part nobody prints — a shaded row of
what not to say. **Job's friends got it wrong for thirty chapters**, and the book
records every word so we would know. *"Everything happens for a reason" is not in
the Bible. "I don't know what to say, but I'm not leaving" is closer to Job 2:13,
where they sat with him seven days and said nothing.*
**Root:** Job 2:13 · Romans 12:15. **Where:** a spread with the letters page.

## 22 · "One man's trash / another's treasure" — radial opinions around one object
**The mechanic:** hundreds of handwritten opinions radiating outward from a single
photographed object in the centre. The noise is the point.

**Ours — the stone.**
An ordinary rock photographed in the centre. Radiating out in handwriting:
everything it was called. *worthless · in the way · just a rock · not worth
carrying · a pillow · a pillar · Bethel · the house of God.* The words nearest the
stone are dismissive; the ones furthest out are what it became.
**Root:** Genesis 28:11 → 28:18 · Psalm 118:22 — *"the stone the builders
rejected has become the cornerstone."* **Where:** a full page.

## 23 · "Note to self: FANTASY = goal without a deadline" — the equation stack
**The mechanic:** stacked equations where each line adds one highlighted term.
You watch a thing become another thing by accumulation.

**Ours — "Faith = ?"**
*BELIEF = agreeing it is true. · FAITH = belief + acting like it. · TRUST = faith +
time. · HOPE = trust + a promise you did not make yourself.* Each term in a
different highlighter, each with its reference.
**Root:** James 2:17 · Hebrews 11:1 · Romans 5:3–4. **Where:** the field guide, 17.

## 24 · "In my control / out of my control" — the closed loop
**The mechanic:** one hand-drawn irregular loop. Inside it, what is yours.
Outside, everything else. The wobble in the line makes it human.

**Ours — "What you are actually carrying."**
Inside the loop: *today · your next sentence · who you tell · whether you go
back.* Outside: *the outcome · other people · the twenty years · whether it makes
sense yet.* Caption from Matthew 6:27 — *"Can all your worries add a single
moment to your life?"*
**Root:** Matthew 6:27, 34. **Where:** near the weather page.

## 25 · "Joy Break Generator" — three columns you combine
**The mechanic:** pick one from column A, one from B, one from C. A generator,
not a list. It makes the reader do something.

**Ours — "Where to start."**
*Column A: a place you already are* (a car · a kitchen at night · a waiting room).
*Column B: one verse* (Psalm 23 · Matthew 11:28 · Genesis 28:15). *Column C: one
minute of what* (read it out loud · say it back angry · write it on your hand).
Any combination is a legitimate start. That is the theology of the page.
**Root:** Deuteronomy 30:14 — *"The message is very close at hand."*
**Where:** facing the notebook page.

## 26 · "January's inner work" / the monthly log
**The mechanic:** a hand-lettered grid you circle, or a page of prompts you fill
in by hand. It becomes yours the moment you mark it.

**Ours — "The month, in your handwriting."**
*Reading · Carrying · Grateful for · Praying the same thing about · Nobody knows
that I · Coming back to.* Rules to write on. The last line pre-filled:
**still here.**
**Root:** Lamentations 3:22–23. **Where:** with the Spine, tear-out.

## 27 · Cleveland Institute — "Please fill in the blanks" (redaction as a form)
**The mechanic:** a printed page where the *answers* are redacted and the
questions sit underneath. You read the shape of a conversation you cannot hear.

**Ours — "The half of the conversation you can hear."**
Prayers with the human half printed and God's half redacted to blocks — then, at
the bottom, one block unredacted: Genesis 28:15. The design argument: you are not
owed the whole transcript, and one line is enough to go on.
**Root:** Genesis 28:15 · Job 38:1–4. **Where:** a full page.

## 28 · "Three-word quotes"
**The mechanic:** a list where the constraint is the format. Short enough that
every entry is memorable.

**Ours — the shortest verses in the Bible.**
*Jesus wept. John 11:35. · God is love. 1 John 4:8. · Pray continually. 1 Thess
5:17. · Rejoice always. · Do not fear.* Set enormous, one per line, references
tiny. The whole page argues that it does not take many words.
**Where:** page 16, or an inside-cover.

## 29 · "Be a Lifeboat" — one drawn object with radiating labels
**The mechanic:** a single hand-drawn object with hand-lettered lines radiating
to every function it serves. Warm, homemade, generous.

**Ours — "Be a stone."**
One drawn rock with lines out to everything it was: *somewhere to put your head ·
proof you were there · a marker · the thing that got poured on · the start of a
house.* Turns the object into a job description for a person.
**Root:** Genesis 28:11, 18 · 1 Peter 2:5 — *"you are living stones."*
**Where:** back of the book.

## 30 · The "razors" series (Occam, Hanlon, Hofstadter, anchoring)
**The mechanic:** one bold rule, one plain-English example, one small drawing.
Consistent template, endlessly repeatable.

**Ours — "Rules for reading the Bible without getting it wrong."**
Same template, six of them. *Read the sentence before it. · If it is a promise,
check who it was said to. · The Bible reports things it does not approve of. ·
A verse is not a fortune cookie. · If it makes you feel superior, re-read it. ·
When in doubt, read it out loud to somebody.*
**Where:** page 02 or the field guide. **Note:** this is the one "framework"
format that earns its place, because it protects the reader from us.

## Still rejected, and why

The strategy frameworks (SWOT/TOWS/PESTEL, Iceberg, Golden Circle, 9 Stages of
Change, Strategic Thinking, Zero-to-Pro), the productivity razors used as
productivity, the journaling-prompt lists, and the vision-board collages. They
are good tools and they belong in **our** workspace. In the paper they would make
us the expert with a system, which is the one thing the constitution says we are
not. Keep them here. Do not print them.
