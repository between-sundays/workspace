Make It Feel Like A Newspaper.

Not a devotional PDF. Not 48 posters. A full Sunday paper with departments, interruptions, tiny discoveries, big ads, quiet Scripture, weird humor, and pages people want to touch.

Art direction reset

The Newspaper Is The Container. The Pages Can Change Clothes.

Use the official logo and warm packaging world as the paper's identity, then let the inside behave like a living newspaper: editorial, funny, visual, dense, quiet, public, and sometimes wildly full-color.

Brand As Object

The logo belongs on the masthead, boxes, delivery van, subscription labels, footer slugs, and issue furniture. It should feel like a real thing in the world, not a filter applied to every page.

Paper Grammar

Front page, columns, classifieds, obits, listings, box scores, captions, maps, weather, tiny ads, pull quotes, corrections, reader letters, and puzzle logic. This is where it starts feeling real.

Holy Interruptions

A dense article can turn into a full-page object ad. A quiet verse can breathe on a nearly empty page. A funny business listing can carry a theological idea without announcing that it is doing so.

Visual menu

Ten Looks The Issue Can Rotate Through.

The old PDF got strong when it stopped acting like one template. This direction builds that into the system: each department has its own native format, but the whole object still feels edited by one paper.

Real Photo

Documentary images of bus stops, laundromats, airports, motel halls, fields, driveways, kitchens, sidewalks. Original, cinematic, but ordinary.

Broadsheet

Big serif headlines, info cards, captions, callouts, tiny maps, and reading routes that make the issue feel like a true newspaper.

White Space

Scripture and small faith pages need quiet. Let a sentence sit with air around it instead of decorating every inch.

Object Ads

Apple and Volvo style: one object, one sentence, one theological turn. Boulder to mustard seed is the clearest example.

Human Marks

Highlighted Scripture, circled phrases, sticky notes, receipts, prayer notes, margins, corrections, and handwritten arrows.

Poster

Full-bleed color, big type, street-sign logic, sports confidence, and back-cover lines that work from across the room.

Illustration

Comics, kid pages, fictional parables, surreal diagrams, and playful cartoons that keep the paper from becoming too self-serious.

Dense Type

Classifieds, sports agate, tiny listings, quarter ads, obits, prayer requests, and all the little things people hunt through.

Product World

Daily Bread Bakery, Stone Pillow Co., Mercy and Sons Moving, Presence Coverage, fictional goods photographed like real brands.

Games

Crosswords, word ladders, spot-the-ladder, maze maps, kid coloring, reader prompts, issue-wide meta clues, and writable space.

Page-turn rhythm

Spread Energy, Not Same Page Energy.

Think in spreads. A dense page should earn a quiet page. A sincere Scripture spread can be followed by a strange ad. A game can reset the room after a heavier section.

48-page flatplan

A Fuller Issue Map With More Kinds Of Pages.

The center folio moves to pages 23-26 so the leave-behind piece is literally central. Scripture lands around 7 pages total, roughly 15 percent of the issue.

Signature spreads

The Pages I Would Push Hardest.

These are the pages that can make Between Sundays feel unlike normal Christian print. They are memorable, ownable, and still tied to the biblical story.

Boulder / Mustard Seed

Run this as a two-page house ad. Page one: "We made faith heavier than Jesus did." Page two: a tiny seed with "Faith, actual size." It uses white space and page turn as theology.

The Spine

Pages 23-26 should feel like a mini newspaper, not an excerpt. Discovery, Scripture, meaning, response. The following page says: "If the middle is missing, good."

Sports Section

Use real sports-page energy: race photos, box scores, stats, standings, and a profile. The faith connection should be endurance and presence, not victory slogans.

Annotated Scripture

One page should feel like a reader attacked the Bible with a highlighter and pen. Circle "I am with you." Draw arrows. Make the text feel encountered, not quoted.

Space strategy

Not Everything Deserves A Full Page.

Maximizing space does not mean filling every page. It means choosing what gets billboard treatment and what becomes the tiny newspaper stuff people love discovering.

Bundle Small Departments

Weather, advice, letters, corrections, and prayer requests can share a spread. It will feel more like a paper and less like a slide deck.

Let Ads Interrupt

Do not group house ads. Place them between sections so they feel like real newspaper ad breaks that also deepen the issue theme.

Use Quarter Ads

Classifieds, now hiring, local fictional businesses, and tiny "found" notices can carry theology in small spaces. Full pages are for the strongest campaigns.

Protect Quiet

Scripture pages can be open and spacious, but not all identical. Alternate columns, single-sentence pages, annotated margins, and large readable passages.

Print critique baked in

Design It Like It Will Actually Hit A Press.

Before this becomes production, the creative concept needs to respect gutters, page turns, image resolution, bleed, trim, folds, ink behavior, and the fact that people hold the paper at arm's length.

Bleed and trimBuild with proper bleed from the beginning. Poster pages and full-bleed photos cannot be afterthoughts.
Gutter logicKeep essential text and faces away from the fold. Use the fold intentionally on the Spine and big spreads.
Image qualityAny real or generated imagery needs press-ready resolution, not browser-export softness.
Type scaleDense newspaper sections can be small, but Scripture and core stories need generous, readable body type.
Color disciplineUse brand colors as recurring identity, then let issue sections bring red, violet, green, cyan, black, and white.
