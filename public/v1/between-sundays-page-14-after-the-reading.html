Before Commentary

The story in Genesis does not ask the reader to pretend the road is easy. Jacob is moving with family history behind him and uncertainty ahead of him. The page does not need to solve all of that. It simply keeps the scene honest: a person on the way, a place that looks ordinary, and a promise that arrives before the person has everything sorted.

Between Sundays works best when it lets that kind of ordinary space stay ordinary. A laundromat. A terminal chair. A borrowed couch. A walk back to the car after a hard conversation. The paper is not trying to make every moment dramatic. It is trying to keep the reader awake to the possibility that God can meet someone there too.

What the story gives you first

  1. A person in transit.Jacob is between where he came from and where he is going.
  2. An ordinary place.The scene starts with sleep, stone, road, and night.
  3. A promise."I am with you" sits inside the passage before the issue says anything else.
  4. A waking sentence."Surely Yahweh is in this place, and I didn't know it."
Read first. Explain slower. No pressure to solve the whole passage in one sitting.
