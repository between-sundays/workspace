In Defense Of Ordinary Places

A short argument against rushing past the room you are already in.

The easiest way to miss a place is to decide too early what it is for. A waiting room becomes only a delay. A hallway becomes only a passage. A city block becomes only the space between two errands.

This issue keeps circling one quieter possibility: the place you almost walked past may still be worth noticing. Not because every detail contains a hidden message, and not because every inconvenience needs to be spiritualized. Simply because attention is one of the first ways a person becomes available to wonder.

The point is not to invent a secret lesson for every room. It is smaller than that. Stay awake enough to notice what hurry would have edited out.

Counterpoint

Not every delay is holy. Some rooms are just hard. The invitation is not to decorate the difficulty. It is to let the room be noticed before it is dismissed.
